# ANGRY FRUIT: THE GATHERING
### 60-Second Proof-of-Concept Teaser · Production Plan

**Format:** 16:9 horizontal, 3840×2160 master, 24 fps, 60.0 seconds
**Purpose:** The teaser that sells a premium limited streaming series. It is not a kids' spot.
**Arc:** suspense → revelation → character comedy → war → silence → title

---

## 0. The Mandate

This premise sounds ridiculous until you see it. The teaser has one job: make that flip happen inside a minute. Somebody laughs at the logline, watches the first twelve seconds, and quits laughing for real before Potato makes them laugh again.

That only works if the craft is real:

- **Play every threat straight.** The camera, the lighting, the score and the actors all believe this war will happen. Nobody mugs at the lens.
- **The comedy comes from design and from character.** A watermelon mortician taking off his gloves is funny because he does it like an undertaker who has buried a lot of friends. No punchlines get inserted. The cast is the punchline.
- **Pork and Beef stay mysterious.** We hear them before we see them. We see shapes before details. Nobody gets a clean look at a face in this teaser. Episode One gets to show that.

---

## 1. The World

**Angry Fruit is the name of the universe, not a casting restriction.** Fruit, vegetables, grains, meat and whatever else the series needs all live here. In this teaser, produce is the population we follow and meat is the weather coming in.

**No humans exist.** No giant kitchens, no human hands, no farmer, no knife from above. Produce is the native scale of the world, and the cities are built for its people. Nothing is shrunk down or made to look cute.

**The history.** Fruit and vegetables have fought for generations over market stalls, irrigation rights, old slurs, and whether Tomato counts as either one. The two sides live in separate districts and go to separate bars. The grudges came down from their parents.

**The turn.** The Pork Tribe and the Beef Tribe are moving off the far ranges. Strawberry is the only one who saw it coming. He's a pirate-radio conspiracy host, and everybody has written him off as a crank. Now the crank is right, and he has to pull together a coalition of people who can't stand each other before the herd reaches the fences.

## 2. Visual Identity: "Orchard Noir"

This is an original look. It is not a copy of any existing franchise, and especially not of the Captain Lemon reference sheet. That sheet is used **only** as a benchmark for how consistent and well-documented each character should be.

### 2.1 Rendering

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Skins** | Macro food photography realism. Strawberry seeds sit in real pits. Avocado skin is pebbled and matte. Watermelon has wax bloom. Grapes have dusty bloom that smears where they touch. Potatoes have dirt in the eyes. Moisture and dew catch light. |
| **Faces** | Faces are sculpted *into* the produce surface, never stuck on top. Brows, cheekbones and lips are raised forms of the skin material. Eyes are glossy, wet and fully rendered, with real catchlights. |
| **Body construction** | The produce *is* the torso and head as one mass. The face sits in the upper third. Arms and legs are slender limbs grown from the plant: green-stem limbs for most characters, root limbs for root vegetables. Hands have four fingers and are often gloved. Feet usually wear shoes or boots. |
| **Wardrobe** | Real tailoring and real cloth simulation: wool pills, leather creases, canvas wears pale at the edges. Clothing is cut for a produce body, so jackets flare around round torsos and collars sit where a neck would be. |
| **Proportion** | Every character has a locked height in **Strawberry Units (SU)**. See 3.0. These never change from shot to shot. |
| **Lens language** | Shoot it like a feature film: anamorphic flares, shallow depth of field, real lens breathing and rack focus. There's a 2.39:1 letterbox inside the 16:9 frame for the body of the teaser, and it opens to full 16:9 for the Money Shot (S21–S23). |
| **Atmosphere** | Always something in the air: ash, pollen, irrigation mist, smoke, dust, chaff, rain. |
| **Grain** | Fine 35mm-style grain in every frame. |

### 2.2 Palette

| Group | Colors | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Produce civilization** | Ripe saturated reds, yellows, oranges and greens (#B3122E, #F2C230, #E8731C, #6E9B3A) under moon-blue (#3A5A8C) and lantern amber (#F5A340) | Alive and lived in, warm in the middle of the dark |
| **The Herd (Pork & Beef)** | Iron (#3B3B3F), char (#1A1614), ember (#FF5A1F), cured-leather brown (#6B3F26) | Heat, smoke, and something that smells like a smokehouse |
| **Bunker** | Tungsten bulb (#FFB46B), paper map cream (#E8DCC0), deep shadow (#0E0C0B) | Conspiracy and late nights |
| **Title** | Pure black, bone-white type | Silence |

### 2.3 Lighting Language

- **Produce world:** motivated sources only (moon, lanterns, cookfires, market neon, sodium streetlights over canals).
- **The Herd:** **always backlit**, edged in ember light through smoke. The front of their faces never gets key light in this teaser.
- **Strawberry:** a hard top or side key that carves one eye and leaves the other in shadow until the final push-in, when both eyes are lit for the first time. That's his arc in one lighting decision.

### 2.4 Type

The title wordmark is a heavy, chiseled serif with cracked edges, like a name carved into an orchard gate and then burned. It's bone-white on black. Taglines are set in a condensed sans in all caps with wide tracking.

## 3. Cast Character Sheets

### 3.0 What every sheet must contain

Every character gets a production sheet at the same standard of documentation as the reference image. **The designs are original.** Nothing is lifted from Captain Lemon or any other existing character.

Each sheet includes:

1. **Turnaround:** front, 3/4 front, profile, 3/4 back and back, in a neutral A-pose against flat mid-grey (#808080).
2. **Expression set (6):** neutral, angry, afraid, deadpan, laughing, the character's signature look.
3. **Hands:** open, fist, holding the signature prop.
4. **Prop callouts:** each prop drawn on its own with dimensions in SU.
5. **Material swatches:** skin macro, each fabric, each metal.
6. **Color chips** with hex values (given below).
7. **Scale lineup:** next to Strawberry for size.
8. **Neutral anchor still:** front-facing medium shot with flat even light. This is the reference image fed into every generation.

**Scale ladder (Strawberry = 1.0 SU):**

|---|---|---|---|
| Grape (each) | 0.25 | Banana | 1.5 |

### 3.2 The Herd: Pork Tribe & Beef Tribe

**Their nature is the series' first mystery.** They read as boar-shaped and bull-shaped warriors wearing cured hide, iron and butcher's tools. What they *are* gets saved for Episode One.

> **[PORK TRIBE]** Massive 4.5-scale boar-shaped warriors seen only as backlit silhouettes, heavy hunched heads, long curved yellowed tusks catching ember light, cured-leather lamellar armor laced with coarse butcher's twine, iron meat hooks and chains hanging from their belts, carrying tall iron brazier-standards trailing smoke, breath steaming in the cold.
>
> **[BEEF TRIBE]** Colossal 6.5-scale bull-shaped warriors seen only as backlit silhouettes, enormous wide-spread horns capped with riveted steel, riveted steel plate armor over hide stamped with glowing ember-orange brand sigils, iron-shod hooves that crack the ground, breath steaming in the cold.

**Reveal rules for this teaser:**

| Allowed | Never |
|---|---|
| Silhouettes through smoke | A frontal face with key light |
| Ember rim light on tusk and horn edges | Readable eyes or mouths |
| Hooves, boots, chains, banners in close-up | A full body in clean light |
| Steam from breath | Any comedy beat |
| Horn and tusk profiles | Dialogue |

The Herd is the one thing in the teaser that is **never funny.**

## 5. Shot-by-Shot Breakdown

**How to read this.** There are 25 numbered shots. When a beat in the script lasts under two seconds, it sits inside a **shot block** (S06, S14, S15) and gets its own sub-shot prompt. **Generate every sub-shot at 3–5 seconds and trim it in the edit.** Generation models fall apart under two seconds. Editors don't.

**Every generation prompt below works on its own.** It carries the full character anchors and the style block. Copy and paste it as written. Attach the matching character's Neutral Anchor Still (3.0, item 8) as an image reference whenever the tool you're using allows it.

**Style block** (already included in every prompt):
*Original stylized 3D animated feature film, premium cinematic quality, anthropomorphic produce characters with photoreal macro food textures, faces sculpted into the skin, slender plant-stem limbs, four-fingered hands, believable cloth simulation, filmic lighting, anamorphic lens, shallow depth of field, volumetric atmosphere, fine 35mm film grain, 16:9, no humans, no text, no watermark.*

**Negative prompt** (use on every shot where the tool supports it):
*human, human hands, kitchen, knife from above, cartoon flat shading, chibi, toy, plastic look, superhero costume, cape, logo, text, subtitles, watermark, extra fingers, distorted face, cute, bright daylight (except S15b).*

## 7. Production Pipeline

1. **Character sheets first.** Build every hero sheet (3.0) and approve it before generating a single shot. Lock the Neutral Anchor Stills.
2. **Environment plates.** Generate keyframes for the four locations and approve them: the Orchard Districts, the Root Cellar, the Old Market Square, the Great Orchard Gate. Then the Far Ranges and the burned plain.
3. **Keyframes.** One still per shot (and per sub-shot) from the prompts above, with the anchor stills attached as references. Approve against the Continuity Lock Sheet.
4. **Image-to-video.** Animate each approved keyframe. Generate 3–5s even for beats under a second.
5. **Edit to the cue sheet** (section 4). Music and the march thud drive the cut points. Picture follows sound.
6. **Voice.** Cast and record dialogue **before** final lip-sync passes. Strawberry needs a calm, low register: authority without volume. Potato needs to be sincere enough to break your heart. Gerald needs to be small and right.
7. **Sound design and mix.** The silences (S01, S17, S20, S25) get mixed with as much care as the explosions. They are where the trailer's power comes from.
8. **Grade.** Moon-blue → ember → tungsten → gold dawn. The color arc tells the story even with the sound off.
9. **Title and type** set last.

## 8. Why It Works

The premise is a joke. The execution can't be.

Each beat is built so the audience laughs **at the right moment and only then**: at Potato, at the onions, at Pumpkin, at Gerald. Everything else is shot as if a real war is coming, because inside this world it is. That tension between a ridiculous premise and a serious world is the whole product. The teaser's job is to prove the show can hold that tension for sixty seconds, so a buyer believes it can hold it for eight episodes.

When the screen goes black and Gerald gets told to shut up, the audience should laugh, and then realize they want to know what the Beef Tribe looks like.

That's Episode One.
